\section*{Brief Project Description}

American Sign Language (ASL) is the primary language for hundreds of thousands
of people in North America, but most hearing people can't understand it. This
project builds a model that takes a short video clip of someone signing and
predicts the English word being signed.

What makes this harder than standard image classification is that ASL is
inherently spatial-temporal: the hand \textit{shape} alone often isn't enough.
For instance, the signs for \textit{drink} (hand tilted to mouth) and
\textit{cool} (fingers fanning near face) look similar in a single frame but
have distinct motions. A CNN-LSTM hybrid handles this naturally: a CNN encodes
what each frame looks like, and an LSTM captures how those representations
change over time. Trying to hand-engineer features robust to different signers,
lighting, and backgrounds is basically infeasible, which is why a learned
deep-learning approach is appropriate.

The model input is a fixed-length sequence of video frames (30 frames per clip),
and the output is a predicted word from a 10-class vocabulary.
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows the overall pipeline.

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/pipeline.png}
  \caption{Model pipeline. A pretrained (frozen) ResNet-50 encodes each of the
           30 sampled frames into a 2048-dim feature, which is projected to 256-dim
           and fed into a 1-layer LSTM (128 hidden units). Global average pooling
           over time feeds a linear softmax classifier.}

\end{figure}

\section*{Data Processing}

\textbf{Dataset.}
I used the WLASL (Word-Level American Sign Language) dataset
\citep{li2020sign}, a large collection of ASL videos sourced from YouTube.
The full dataset has over 2000 words, but I narrowed it down to 10 words to
keep training feasible: \textit{drink, computer, before, go, who, cousin, help,
thin, cool, tall}. I chose these because they had the highest number of
actually downloadable videos in the top-100 word subset.

\textbf{Original WLASL Cleaning.}
The dataset comes with a JSON file of YouTube links and timestamps.
A large fraction of those links were dead (404 or geo-blocked), so I had to
filter them. After removing broken links and clips shorter than 10 frames, I
was left with only \textbf{147 usable videos} from WLASL---about 15 per
class---with pre-defined 105/26/16 train/val/test splits.
This meant only 1--3 test examples per class, making evaluation essentially
meaningless.

\textbf{Expanding with YouTube Data.}
To address the data scarcity problem, I supplemented WLASL with additional
ASL tutorial videos sourced directly from YouTube.  For each of the 10 target
words I defined four search queries (e.g.\ ``ASL drink sign language,''
``how to sign drink in ASL'') and retrieved candidate videos using yt-dlp.
I then downloaded each candidate with pytubefix and applied a quality filter:
only clips in which MediaPipe Hands detected at least one hand in
${\ge}8$ of the 30 sampled frames were accepted. In total I collected
\textbf{507 additional YouTube clips}, giving a combined dataset of
\textbf{654 videos}. Table~\ref{tab:class_dist} shows the per-class counts.
I re-created stratified 70/15/15 train/val/test splits, yielding
\textbf{456 / 98 / 100} videos respectively.

\textbf{Preprocessing.}
All frames are resized to $224{\times}224$ pixels and uniformly sampled to 30
frames per video (shorter clips are looped; longer ones are subsampled). Frames
are normalized with ImageNet mean and standard deviation to match the pretrained
ResNet-50 input distribution. ResNet-50 features (2048-dim per frame) are
cached after extraction.

Figure~\ref{fig:sample_frames} shows example preprocessed frames from three
classes, with MediaPipe hand landmarks overlaid.

  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/sample_frames_landmarks.png}
  \caption{Sample cleaned frames ($224{\times}224$) from the training set,
           with MediaPipe Hands landmarks drawn. Rows (top to bottom):
           \textit{drink}, \textit{before}, \textit{cousin}.}
  \label{fig:sample_frames}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Challenges.}
Mixing WLASL (professional, curated) with YouTube tutorials (diverse signers,
backgrounds, camera distances) introduces domain shift. YouTube ASL tutorial
videos vary considerably in how much of the signer is visible, lighting
conditions, and signing speed. The MediaPipe hand-presence filter
(${\ge}8/30$ frames) rejects clips showing only faces or backgrounds, but
within-class variance is still high.

\section*{Baseline Model}

My baseline extracts hand landmarks with Google's MediaPipe Hands
\citep{zhang2020mediapipe}, then classifies with an SVM---a
``hand-crafted features + classical ML'' approach to compare against the
learned CNN-LSTM.

\textbf{Feature extraction.}
For each video I run the MediaPipe HandLandmarker on every one of the 30
sampled frames. MediaPipe returns 21 3D landmarks per hand (x, y, z), giving a
63-element vector per frame. I average these vectors across all detected frames
to get one fixed-size 63-dim feature per video. This deliberately discards
temporal information---the point is to establish a no-sequence baseline.

\textbf{Training.}
I used an RBF-kernel SVM (class weights balanced) and ran 5-fold cross-validation
grid search over C $\in$ \{0.1, 1, 10, 100\} and $\gamma \in$ \{scale, 0.01, 0.001\}
on the training set. Best hyperparameters: \textbf{C = 100, $\gamma$ = scale}.

\textbf{Quantitative results.}
CV accuracy on the training set: 22.8\%.
\textbf{Val accuracy: 24.5\%};
\textbf{test accuracy: 26.0\%} (26 out of 100 correct; random chance = 10\%).
Table~\ref{tab:baseline_acc} shows per-class test accuracy.

\textbf{Qualitative results.}
The SVM's performance dropped substantially when evaluated on the combined
dataset compared to results on WLASL alone. The averaged-landmark feature works
well on curated WLASL videos where signers are consistent, but struggles on the
mixed YouTube data with variable backgrounds, camera angles, and signing styles.
Only \textit{help} (44\%) and \textit{before} (33\%) exceed 30\%---both signs
have distinctive static hand configurations that remain recognizable despite
signer variability.

\textbf{Challenges.}
The averaging operation discards temporal information. More importantly, the
SVM's performance is strongly affected by domain shift: MediaPipe landmark
features from a controlled WLASL video look different from those in a casual
YouTube tutorial.

\section*{Primary Model}

\textbf{Architecture.}
The model is a CNN-LSTM implemented in PyTorch
(Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). A frozen pretrained ResNet-50
(\texttt{torchvision.models.ResNet50\_Weights.IMAGENET1K\_V2}) encodes each
frame to 2048 dimensions. ResNet features are cached so they only need to be
computed once. The sequence of 30 feature vectors is projected to 256 dimensions
(ReLU), then fed into a 1-layer LSTM with 128 hidden units. Rather than just
using the final hidden state, I global-average-pool across all time steps before
the linear classifier. Total trainable parameters: \textbf{723,466}.

\textbf{Training.}
Adam optimizer (lr $= 5\times10^{-4}$, weight decay $= 5\times10^{-3}$),
batch size 8, class-weighted cross-entropy, cosine annealing LR schedule.
Gaussian noise ($\sigma = 0.05$) is added to ResNet features during training
as a feature-level augmentation. Trained for 100 epochs; best checkpoint
selected by validation accuracy.

\textbf{Quantitative results.}
Best \textbf{val accuracy: 35.7\%} (epoch 14).
\textbf{Test accuracy: 31.0\%} (31 out of 100 correct).
The CNN-LSTM \textbf{outperforms the SVM baseline} (31\% vs.\ 26\%) on the
expanded dataset, reversing the result observed on the smaller WLASL-only data.
Figure~\ref{fig:learning_curves} shows the training and validation accuracy
curves.

\textbf{Qualitative results.}
With more training data, \textit{computer} (89\%) and \textit{thin} (64\%)
are now well-classified. The model still struggles with \textit{drink} (0\%)
and \textit{cool} (0\%) whose ResNet features overlap across classes when the
background is variable.

\textbf{Key finding.}
With only 147 videos (WLASL alone), the SVM's 63 hand-geometry features were
far more sample-efficient than the 723K-parameter CNN-LSTM: SVM 56\% vs
CNN-LSTM 38\%. After expanding to 654 videos via YouTube supplementation, the
CNN-LSTM (31\%) now \textbf{exceeds} the SVM (26\%), confirming that data
scarcity was the primary bottleneck. Absolute test accuracy decreased for both
models due to the domain shift introduced by YouTube data, but the CNN-LSTM
is proportionally more robust.

\section*{Improved Primary Model}

\textbf{Data expansion experiments.}
The previous section demonstrated that the CNN-LSTM benefits more from
additional data than the SVM. In this section I document the full experimental
trajectory, including results on the original WLASL-only dataset and the
subsequent improvement from YouTube data augmentation.

\textbf{Phase 0: WLASL-only small-data baseline (147 videos).}
Before YouTube expansion, I ran three experiments on the original 147-video
dataset. These are summarised briefly as context.

\textbf{Experiment A --- End-to-end ResNet layer4 fine-tuning.}
I cached ResNet-50 up to \texttt{layer3} and fine-tuned \texttt{layer4} +
a tiny LSTM (64 hidden units) end-to-end. Fine-tuning 12M backbone parameters
on only 105 training clips caused catastrophic overfitting.
\textbf{Result: ensemble test accuracy 18.8\% (3/16).}

\textbf{Experiment B --- Per-frame Landmark-LSTM.}
I trained a two-layer LSTM directly on per-frame MediaPipe landmark sequences
($(147\times30\times63)$ array), preserving temporal information the SVM
discards. MediaPipe missed hands in ${\sim}40\%$ of frames, and with only
10--11 training clips per class the LSTM could not learn meaningful temporal
patterns. \textbf{Result: ensemble test accuracy 18.8\% (3/16).}

\textbf{Experiment C --- SVM + CNN-LSTM probability ensemble (WLASL only).}
I re-trained the SVM with Platt scaling and combined its probabilities with
the Phase-1 LSTM. The best combined weight ($w_\text{SVM}{=}0.8$) reached
50\% test accuracy (8/16) but could not surpass the 56.2\% SVM alone.

\textbf{Discussion.}
The core finding is that \textit{data quantity matters more than model complexity}
in this task. Table~\ref{tab:comparison} summarises how results changed between
the 147-video and 654-video settings.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{Summary: test accuracy (\%) before and after YouTube data expansion.
           The CNN-LSTM's advantage over SVM emerges once sufficient data is available.}
  \label{tab:comparison}
  \begin{tabular}{lccc}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Model} & \textbf{WLASL only (N=147)} & \textbf{WLASL+YouTube (N=654)} \\
    \midrule
    SVM           & 56.2\%                       & 26.0\%  \\
    CNN-LSTM      & 37.5\%                       & 31.0\%  \\
    Ensemble      & 50.0\%                       & \textbf{34.0\%} \\
    \midrule
    Test set size & 16                           & 100 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Both models' absolute accuracy decreased after data expansion, because
YouTube tutorials introduce domain shift (varied backgrounds, camera distances,
non-standard signing contexts) that neither model was pre-adapted to handle.
The SVM suffered more severely (-30 pp) than the CNN-LSTM (-7 pp), because
averaged landmark features are highly sensitive to camera framing and signer
distance while the CNN-LSTM's ResNet features are more viewpoint-robust.
Consequently, the CNN-LSTM now outperforms the SVM (31\% vs 26\%), a reversal
of the small-data result.

\textbf{Remaining challenges.}
Absolute accuracy of 34\% is still modest (random chance: 10\%); three issues
remain: (1)~\textbf{Domain shift.}  WLASL and YouTube videos differ in
background, camera angle, and signing style; a domain adaptation step or
training on consistently framed data would help.
(2)~\textbf{ResNet features are not hand-specific.}  ResNet-50 was trained on
ImageNet; its 2048-dim representation encodes background and face appearance
alongside hand shape. Replacing it with a video-level foundation model
(e.g.\ VideoMAE \citep{tong2022videomae} pretrained on Kinetics-400) or
first cropping to detected hand regions would remove irrelevant dimensions.
(3)~\textbf{Label quality.}  YouTube search results for ``ASL X sign language''
sometimes return vocabulary overview videos or non-standard signing, introducing
label noise even after the MediaPipe quality filter.

\textbf{Next steps.}
To further improve: (1) apply hand-crop preprocessing with MediaPipe before
feature extraction, giving the CNN a stable, centred view of the signing hand
irrespective of camera distance; (2) use a video foundation model backbone
(VideoMAE) that is pre-trained on temporal data; (3) gather more per-word
data by broadening the search queries or using multiple per-word YouTube
channels known to produce high-quality isolated-sign content.
